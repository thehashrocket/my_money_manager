# my_money_manager

Local-first, single-user personal budgeting app for Star One Credit Union (checking + savings), plus the credit cards and loans you owe on. Transactions arrive on their own over SimpleFIN, or from a CSV export when you need older history. Categorize them, track envelope-style budgets, watch your net worth, and keep every row on your own machine instead of handing it to Plaid or a cloud service.

**Status:** v1.6.1. **A fund could be quietly nudged toward tracking real money moved instead of money planned — the thing its whole design promises it never does — and two separate ways in are now closed.** Reclassify a category to a fund and any rule trained on it beforehand kept working, so an import could still match a withdrawal through that old rule straight into the fund; import-time matching now refuses a fund on either sign of transaction, not just deposits. Separately, undoing a categorize action after the transaction's old category had been reclassified to a fund (another tab, or a fast second click) put the row back there anyway with no import or rule involved — undo now checks the category's current kind first and lands in Uncategorized instead. Whether a fund can ever track real transferred money is now a settled "no, on purpose": `/goals` no longer describes fund progress as temporary, and it says plainly that a fund has no way to represent spending from it and being replenished later — that's an ordinary expense with no link back to the fund.

v1.6.0: **Navigating between pages is now instant.** The app adopts Next.js's Cache Components model: once you've visited a page, coming back to it — Sync back to Dashboard back to Sync, say — shows it immediately instead of re-fetching everything from scratch, while account balances, budget figures and the categorize backlog count still stay live and correct on every real visit. Also fixed: a handful of screens (`/sync`'s banners, a saved card or reconcile form's confirmation, a new-category form's error, a goal-target save's status) could show a stale success or error message left over from a previous visit instead of a clean slate, now that pages stay alive in the background rather than being torn down when you navigate away.

Six helper and warning messages on `/goals`, `/subscriptions`, `/sync`, and `/import` that had been sized like fine print now read at full body size. The left rail's account balance list, whose no-truncation-needed design relied on the old sizing, now truncates a long account name with an ellipsis instead of risking a collision with the balance next to it.

v1.5.0: **Credit cards can now carry a monthly paydown goal.** "Card details" on `/accounts` gained a third field, Monthly paydown goal, next to Credit limit and Minimum payment — a standing amount you're aiming to pay down each month. It's independent of the required minimum payment (a different number for most cards) and never touches budget envelopes or funds — just a goal compared against what you actually paid.

v1.4.2: **On `/transactions`, "Categorize all N →" could promise more rows than `/categorize` could actually file.** With a merchant filtered and "Show transfers" turned on, the count included transfer-paired uncategorized rows — but `/categorize` never shows those and refuses to file them. The button now drops the number and reads "Categorize this merchant →" whenever that mismatch is possible, instead of overpromising.

v1.4.1: **Running the app in Docker could silently lose your database backups over time.** Every container restart took a fresh snapshot and trimmed old ones down to the last 10 — even an ordinary restart with no schema change at all, so ten restarts after any real upgrade, that upgrade's own backup could get pushed out by backups of nothing happening. Snapshots and cleanup now only run when a database upgrade is actually about to happen. And a backup that failed partway (a permissions problem on the backups folder, say) used to print a warning and boot anyway, including right before an upgrade with no working backup behind it — since a backup is now only taken right before an upgrade, a failed one means exactly what it says, so the app refuses to start and tells you how to fix it, keeping any partial file it left behind out of the way of your good backups.

v1.4.0: **`/categorize` can now be scoped to a single month.** Navigate to "just September" (or any other month, via prev/next arrows) to clear that month's backlog without touching the rest of your history. "Categorize all N →" files exactly the rows shown for the month you're viewing — never the merchant's whole history — and the backlog counter and progress ("N of M merchants done") follow whichever scope you're looking at. Switch back to "all time" at any point; a merchant's disclosure panel and its "See all N transactions" link always show the full history regardless of scope, so you can still see how it was categorized in the past.

v1.3.5: **A newly-linked SoFi checking account showed every transaction with no merchant name.** Sync trusts SimpleFIN's `memo` field whenever it's present and only falls back to the feed's `description` when `memo` is missing entirely — but SoFi sends `memo` as a present, empty string on every row, so that fallback never ran and every SoFi transaction's merchant name came back blank. One already-imported Citi Bank row hit the same underlying bug and has been repaired the same way (re-imported with its real merchant text); it'll need re-categorizing, since re-import doesn't carry over the prior category.

v1.3.4: **Reconciling a credit card to a positive balance no longer silently flips it negative.** A card can genuinely be owed money rather than owing it, after an overpayment — but the Reconcile form had no way to say which direction a typed amount went, so it always assumed you owed. Fixed along the way: the toggle could silently revert to "You owe" on a second save even though nothing had changed, re-clicking the already-selected option could freeze the amount field on a stale figure if a charge landed on the card while the form stayed open, and a screen reader described the amount field's direction backwards.

v1.3.3: **The "Move filed rows to another category" tool's Remember checkbox can no longer promise something it wasn't going to do.** Like the checkboxes on `/categorize` and each `/transactions` row, it now checks first whether training or removing a rule actually makes sense for the merchant — greying itself out and explaining why when it doesn't, instead of always offering "Remember" with no explanation and only warning after a refused save. A line telling you Remember would "retrain the merchant's rule" no longer stays on screen when the checkbox is actually about to remove a rule instead. And the checkbox itself — its markup, its disabled look, its label — now comes from one shared implementation across all three surfaces (a transaction row, `/categorize`, and this bulk-move tool) instead of a third hand-copied version.

v1.3.2: **The "Remember" checkbox on `/categorize` and `/transactions` now runs on one shared implementation instead of two independently-maintained copies** — the two had already drifted once, with a fix landing on one page and not the other. Two more ways a ticked box could silently do the wrong thing are fixed along with the consolidation: tick "Remove conflicting rule," change your mind, then land back on a choice that would do the same thing, and the box used to re-check itself with no new click — consent is now cleared for good the instant what the checkbox would actually do changes, instead of just being hidden until you happen to return to the same choice. And a tick could carry over to a different category or merchant than the one you confirmed — a bulk move repointing which category a row would train, or a background merchant-name cleanup changing which merchant it trains against — both now clear the tick instead of submitting it against the new target.

v1.3.0: **`/transactions` now tells you up front when "Remember" won't work, instead of letting you find out after you save.** `/categorize` already greyed out that checkbox when a merchant's history was too mixed to train a rule from (v0.20.0, below); `/transactions` does the same now, with the reason shown right there instead of a warning after the fact. And when a merchant already has a rule that contradicts the category you just picked, both pages now offer to remove it instead of only refusing — the checkbox relabels to **"Remove conflicting rule"** and stays clickable, so a rule that would otherwise keep auto-filing every future transaction the wrong way can finally be taken out from the row where you noticed it, even though checking it can't train a new one in its place. Also fixed: correcting the only transaction you'd seen so far for a merchant no longer permanently blocks training a rule from that very correction; a "Remember" tick can no longer silently carry over to a different category or transaction than the one you confirmed it for; and Save now accepts a pending rule removal even when you haven't also changed the row's category.

v1.2.2: **A hand-entered credit card charge is no longer silently double-counted once you connect that card to your bank.** If you'd typed in a card's charges by hand before linking it to SimpleFIN, the bank's own record of one of those same charges could arrive later as a second, separate row — the date and description rarely match closely enough for the app to recognize it as the same event. Syncing a card like that now stops and tells you to remove the hand-entered charges first, instead of importing anything for it that run; a real gap for anyone who did that, though it never had a chance to happen on this app's own ledger. Also fixed: a quick allocation edit on `/budget` followed immediately by leaving the page could leave the dashboard and `/goals` showing the figure from just before that edit — the page was telling them to refresh before the edit had actually finished saving, and now waits for every in-flight edit to land first. And a compile-time guardrail now catches a dropped refresh-failure warning before it ships, the same class of bug v0.27.0 (below) fixed by hand in forty-seven places.

v1.2.1: **The dashboard could show numbers from before your last budget edit.** Allocate money to a category, copy last month's budget forward, rename or archive a category, or change a rollover setting, then go Home — the "This month" summary and "Closest to limit" tile could still be showing the figures from before that change, until something unrelated happened to refresh them. Every one of those actions now tells the dashboard to catch up, so it reflects what you just did.

v1.2.0: **A checking payment can now be linked straight to the real card charge it paid off.** v1.1.0 (below) brought a linked card's own transactions in, but left a gap: once a card imports its own rows, "Mark as payment to" refuses on it — the bank's own credit is coming, so a hand-made copy would double it — so a checking withdrawal you had already filed under a category had no way back to the card side at all. A new **"Link to a card charge"** item on the `/transactions` row menu lets you pick the real bank row it matches, cross-date: a Citi payment measured 1-3 days off the charge it settles, which is why nothing here waits for an automatic match. Linking removes both rows from spending, the same as any other transfer, and warns rather than blocks when the checking side was already categorized — that category's spend total is about to change, but the link is still the right next step. Also fixed: **"Not a card payment" only appears where it can actually do something.** It used to show up on every paired transaction and fail with the same message on an ordinary bank-to-bank transfer; now it's gated to pairs this app actually created.

v1.1.0: **A linked credit card now imports its own transactions from your bank, instead of just its balance.** This reverses half of the decision v1.0.0 (below) wrote down. Once a card is linked and reconciled, every charge and payment your bank reports comes in and counts toward a category the same way a checking or savings transaction does — spending on that card now shows up in your envelopes. The card's balance stops being read from the bank the moment this starts: it's computed the same way a checking account's is, from your last reconcile plus everything imported since, so keeping it current is on you from here, same as any other reconciled account. History doesn't come along for the ride — only transactions dated after the card's own starting point import, on purpose, so a payment you already recorded by hand and the charge history underneath it are never counted twice. A hand-typed charge or "Mark as payment to" this card is refused rather than silently duplicated once its own rows start arriving, and the row menu stops offering the option at all on a card that will always decline it. Nothing changes for a card that isn't linked, or isn't on your bank's feed to begin with — it still takes hand-entered charges exactly as before. What's still open is written down in [TODOS.md](./TODOS.md) rather than unknown.

v1.0.0: **1.0 is a marker, not a feature release — the app was the same one 0.27.0 shipped a few hours earlier.** What changed is that the last thing 1.0 was waiting on turned out to be a decision rather than a bug, and it was made: **a credit card's balance kept coming from your bank, and card spending stayed outside your envelopes.** A card could have one or the other — the moment it held a charge you typed in by hand, the app stopped reading that card's balance from the bank and handed it to you to keep up to date — and this app picked the automatic balance. So a purchase on the card showed up as what you owed going up, not as spending in Groceries. That is what it had been doing all along; it was written down as a choice, with the reasoning in [PLAN.md](./PLAN.md). v1.1.0 above revisits this for a linked card specifically — an unlinked card, or one your bank doesn't expose over SimpleFIN at all, still works exactly as this entry describes.

v0.27.0: **A change you saved is never reported as a failure.** After saving, the app refreshes the page behind it, and that refresh can fail on its own — when it did, you were told the save had failed, or shown an error page saying nothing had been written, while the change was already in your ledger. Six pages had that problem in forty-seven places; all of them now report a save that landed as a save that landed, with a note that the page may be showing the state from before it. Three were worse than a stale page: reconciling a card balance reported failure and the obvious retry spent that account's single Undo slot on a figure that was never current; importing a CSV said "Nothing was written" several hundred rows in; and categorizing a merchant threw away the ten-second Undo for a write that may have just deleted one of your trained rules. Also: **a hand-entered card charge can be removed** — it was the only thing in the app with no way back — and **you can add one to a card whose balance comes from the bank**, which you could not, so a mistyped credit limit could not be fixed without disconnecting the account first. And the one-click banner that repairs "Left to Budget cannot be computed" works again; it had been refused on every category it could offer since v0.26.0.

v0.26.0: **Re-linking a bank account while a sync is running can no longer put transactions on the wrong account, or overwrite the wrong balance.** Every account is re-checked at the moment of writing, and one whose connection moved is skipped with a message naming it and what to do. A sync that could safely import nothing no longer creates an empty import record — which used to become the Undo target and quietly strand the undo for the last sync that had actually imported something. Messages about withheld transactions now survive closing the tab, two syncs at once no longer fail the whole run, and changing a category to income from its `⋯` menu now asks first, on the server, so a page left open cannot do it by accident.

v0.25.0: **Nothing you can see changes.** A stored figure the app had stopped using was removed from the database. Listed because it moves a column, not because it moves a number.

v0.24.0: **A category's `⋯` menu stops offering changes the app will refuse**, and five fixes to Funds: money paid *into* a fund no longer appears from nowhere in its carried balance, "Planned to date" stops at the month you are looking at instead of counting future allocations, archived funds are no longer offered dead-end actions, a duplicate fund name says so, and edits to one month now refresh the months after it.

v0.23.0: **Two things you could not do before: put money into a fund, and fix a merchant you filed to the wrong category.** Funds showed up on `/budget` but were read-only, and the page they sent you to for contributions never had a contribution form — so across v0.18.0-v0.21.0 a fund could be created and then never funded, by any route in the app. The Funds band is now editable exactly like Income and Expenses: type an amount and it counts against Left to budget the same way an expense does, because putting money aside is what it costs you this month. Each row shows what you have put in from the fund's first month through the one you are looking at, and how far that is from its target, next to the field you type in rather than on another page, and rows carry rollover the way expense rows do. `/goals` keeps targets and the long view, now headlined by what you have actually contributed with money taken back out on its own line, and links straight to the current month's band. One noun throughout: Funds. The web address is still `/goals`, so bookmarks keep working. Separately, filing a whole merchant to the wrong category used to be repairable one transaction at a time once the ten-second undo expired — and `/categorize` stops listing a merchant the moment it is filed, so the mistake became invisible exactly where it was made. The repair now lives on the merchant's own transaction list (`/transactions`, via "See all N transactions"): pick what the rows are wrongly under, pick where they go, move all of them at once, with its own undo and with anything you have since re-filed by hand left alone. Ticking **Remember** on that move can now *retrain* the merchant's rule rather than removing it, because a move that puts the merchant's whole history in one place is the moment the app can be most confident what the rule should say. Also fixed: categorizing a transaction now refreshes the Funds page and the dashboard instead of leaving them showing figures computed before your change.

v0.22.0: **Pressing "Not a reversal" on `/sync` can no longer link the pair instead.** The two buttons on a reversal review card do opposite things to your money: one pairs a charge with its own cancellation and takes both out of every spending total, the other records that they are unrelated and leaves them counted. The form carried which button you pressed, but treated a missing answer as "link" — so any submission that lost that one field paired the two transactions, wiped out any earlier "not a pair" decision you had recorded about them, and reported success. Both buttons now state their answer outright, and a missing answer is refused rather than guessed. The rest of the release is `/sync` telling you it heard the click: every button that changes something greys out and says what it is doing ("Linking…", "Recording…", "Undoing…", "Saving…"), the dropdowns that decide *which two transactions get paired* freeze while a pairing is in flight so a refusal can never appear underneath a selection you never submitted, and on a card with two buttons only the one you pressed reads as busy. Confirmation messages on this page also appear at all now — every one of them was being discarded a fraction of a second before it could be drawn, and rejecting a reversal produced no confirmation anywhere — and three refusals you are meant to read stopped refreshing themselves off the screen before you could. Screen readers are now told that something is in progress instead of going silent for the length of a bank round-trip.

v0.21.0: **Re-pointing a bank connection can no longer duplicate your transactions.** Every synced row now records which bank feed sent it, and nothing erases that. Before this, pointing an account at a different feed wiped the only record of where each already-imported row came from, so the next sync could not tell those rows from new ones and imported the whole overlapping window again — every amount counted twice, no error, no warning, and the only sign would have been your balances quietly drifting away from the bank's. Duplicate detection is now scoped to the feed that issued a transaction id rather than to whichever local account happens to hold the row, which is what a bank's id is actually unique within. Day to day nothing changes — the same rows are recognised as duplicates as before — and re-connecting a feed no longer rewrites a single transaction. What is new is what `/sync` tells you when you change a link: it names transactions from that feed already filed under a different account, which sync will correctly refuse to import again but which leave the new account's balance short until you move them, and its warning about older un-tagged rows no longer misses half of them or understates what happens next. That release carries a database migration — see its upgrade note in [CHANGELOG.md](./CHANGELOG.md).

v0.20.0: **"Remember" now refuses to save a rule it knows would be wrong.** Ticking Remember while categorizing saves a rule that files every future transaction carrying that merchant name, without asking again — so a rule trained on the wrong kind of name is quiet from then on. Two kinds are now refused. The first is a name that is not a merchant: your bank labels online and mobile transfers with nothing but the channel, so `ONLINE` appears on 39 unrelated payments across the whole ledger (a prescription, a Taco Bell reimbursement, money from a refinance) and one rule over it would file every future transfer the same way. The second is a real merchant you have already filed two ways: `AMAZON` and `COSTCO WHSE` are each split across two categories on a real ledger, and no single rule can be right for both. On `/categorize` the checkbox greys itself out with the reason *before* you click, including the moment you create the split by picking a second category; on `/transactions`, where the page doesn't know the merchant's filing history per row, a warning explains it right after. Only the rule is withheld — the transactions are still categorized, because your decision about *those* rows is fine even when generalizing it is not. A refusal can also remove the rule the merchant already had, because a wrong rule keeps filing every future import, which keeps the merchant off `/categorize` entirely, and there is no rules screen to fix it from — so leaving it standing made it permanent. That removal is deliberately narrow: it only happens when the category you picked *contradicts* the rule (picking the category the rule already points at leaves it alone), only from Remember on `/categorize` or a `/transactions` row (never from "Categorize all" on `/subscriptions`), and the notification names the category the removed rule pointed at with an Undo that puts it back exactly as it was. This affects 5 of 180 merchant groups; the other 175 are untouched. Also fixed: a merchant that picks up new uncategorized rows mid-session (an import, a `/sync`, an undo in another tab) reappears on `/categorize` instead of staying hidden and still counted as done; `/subscriptions` reports what its Categorize buttons actually did instead of reporting nothing; and transactions with a blank bank memo can be categorized at all, which they could not.

v0.19.0: two things that had been quietly disagreeing now agree. First, "spent" means the same thing on every page: a refund reduces that category's spend, which is what an envelope budget implies — return $20 of groceries and you have $20 of groceries to buy again. `/budget` already worked that way; the dashboard's 6-month trend chart counted only money going out, so September 2026 showed **$10.00** of Misc on one page and **$295.00** on the other. Same category, same month. The chart now uses the same signed sum, negatives draw below the axis instead of being painted over their neighbours, and spending is decided by the category's kind rather than by which way the amount points (the old sign filter was also, silently, the only thing keeping 43 paychecks worth **+$52,131.17** out of the chart). Second, `/sync` can now pair a charge with its own cancellation. A reversed transfer, a disputed charge with a provisional credit, a returned payment — these put both halves on *one* account, and every matcher in the app required a pair to span two, so nothing could see them and there was no way to fix one by hand. A new **"Reversals needing review"** queue lists them beside the existing transfer review. It never links anything on its own, and that is the point: the same shape turns up as pure coincidence on a real ledger (a $3.99 ATM fee refund opposite a real $3.99 Apple charge), so each one is your call. A **Not a reversal** button dismisses a coincidence without linking it first, and the dismissal sticks. Also fixed: a disputed charge on a credit card is no longer reported as debt you paid off.

v0.18.0: the merchant name on `/categorize` finally goes somewhere. `AMAZON` is a deliberately lossy key — 59 charges can hide behind it — and the only way to see what you were actually filing was to remember the name, open `/transactions` in another tab, and type it again. Now the name opens in place: up to three of the bank's own memo lines for that group (the text that tells `AMZN MKTP US*2X4RT9KL3` apart from `AMAZON.COM*RT4YU8QQ3`), plus a link to every transaction behind the key, already-filed ones included. That link lands on `/transactions` filtered on the exact merchant — not a substring search, which on 11 of 181 groups quietly returns more rows than the group you clicked — led by a removable chip and a line that answers the question you clicked to ask: `50 rows, 1 uncategorized · 49 filed as Gas`. From there, "Categorize all 53" takes you back to bulk filing, and `/categorize` now shows how far you've got (`12 of 181 merchants done`). Your half-finished category pick survives the round trip, hard reload included, and is discarded when the tab closes. Both transaction lists became ruled rows with column headers that line up with the values under them, instead of stacked cards, and every row now shows the bank's own memo text rather than a tooltip that had been displaying the word `WITHDRAWAL` on every row since it shipped.

v0.17.0: merchant names finally group. A reference number, a timestamp or a city stuck on the end of a bank memo used to make every purchase look like a brand-new merchant — 516 different names for 1540 transactions. The normalizer now splits payment processors (`SQ *`, `TST *`) from the merchant behind them, strips known cities and store numbers, and keeps cleaning until the name stops changing, which brought that to 363 names and cut one-off merchants from 354 to 222. Google One and YouTube Premium are separate merchants again instead of sharing one "GOOGLE" bucket that spanned two categories, Amazon Prime stays separate from ordinary Amazon orders, and a store name the bank truncated mid-word (`TAPPED APPLE LL`) no longer loses its last two letters to a state-code strip. Because the merchant name is stored on each row at import time, changing the normalizer leaves your ledger a generation behind the code: `pnpm db:backfill-merchants` closes that gap, renormalizing every row and rewriting the categorization rules you have already trained so they keep firing. It dry-runs by default, snapshots before it writes, and refuses outright when two rules would merge while disagreeing about the category.

v0.16.0: credit cards and loans are real account types — the app has always tracked what you have, and this is the first release that tracks what you owe and puts the two together into a net worth figure. A new `/accounts` page (second in the nav) lists assets and liabilities as ruled lists, with a `Cash` subtotal, a `Debt` subtotal, and net worth closing it; a card row shows how much of its limit is used, when its balance was last confirmed, and how much you paid down this month. You enter what you owe as a positive number and never type a minus sign anywhere in the app — the balance is stored negative, which is what lets every existing balance, transfer-pair, and spending query keep working unchanged. A card's balance moves three ways: **Reconcile** (set what it actually owes today), a hand-entered charge or refund (categorized, so card spending counts toward its envelope in the month you spent it), and marking the checking-side payment as a **card payment** from the `/transactions` row menu, which pairs the two so it counts as neither spending nor income. Feed-linked accounts with no transactions of their own get **Refresh** instead, pulling the balance straight from the bank. Every balance change records the one it replaced and can be undone — and the undo is itself undoable. A mortgage is deliberately quiet: it counts toward net worth, keeps a balance control so it stays correctable, and otherwise stays out of the way. Also new: a **"Closest to limit"** tile on the dashboard (the five categories nearest to spending their budget, worst first), a **show-transfers toggle** on `/transactions`, and `DESIGN.md`'s 4/8/12/16/20/28/40/56 spacing cadence finally applied app-wide across 37 files instead of the 24px shadcn cadence it tells you to avoid.

`/transactions` has a real filter bar: search by description/merchant/payee, narrow to one account or category, pick an arbitrary date range (replacing the old one-month-at-a-time picker) with a "This month" quick-select, filter by amount range regardless of deposit/withdrawal sign, and show posted-only, pending-only, or everything — filters compose and survive pagination. A tenth filter, `?merchant=`, pins the list to one exact merchant key; it has no input in the panel (the key is normalized, so typing `amazon` would return nothing) and arrives instead from a `/categorize` drilldown or a click on any row's merchant name, rendered as a chip you can drop on its own. `/budget`'s category drilldown links were updated to match (they used to jump into that new date-range filter with the old month-picker params, which silently widened to "every transaction ever" instead of erroring). `/budget/[year]/[month]` is a real zero-based (EveryDollar-style) budget: categories have a `kind` (income/expense/fund), every planned-amount cell is editable in place with instant save, "Copy previous month" fills the whole month in one click, and categories can be created, renamed, reorganized, archived, and (in one narrow, evidence-backed case) reclassified from expense to income — all without leaving the page; unarchiving is the one exception, handled from its own `/budget/categories` page. The page also has an in-page "How this page works" reference — a collapsible glossary (closed by default, same native `<details>`/`<summary>` pattern as `/goals`) explaining the envelope-budgeting model and each Left to Budget state, hidden on a genuine first-run month so it never stacks with the onboarding card. Dashboard, bulk categorization, subscriptions, funds and the 6-month trend chart all ship too. `/sync` pulls posted transactions straight from the bank, and its balance check now tells real drift from a stale bank figure instead of flagging both the same way; `/import` still handles anything the feed's 45-day window no longer reaches, and can now fix a wrong starting-balance anchor inline — the anchor move itself is now atomic with the import, bounds-checked against calendar-invalid dates (now enforced on every imported row's date, not just the anchor), and reversible from the import success page, whose "transfer pairs linked" count is now accurate even when a pending row posts via re-import rather than a fresh insert. Transfer-pair matching no longer selects a still-pending row as a match, and a "Not a transfer" correction now sticks instead of being silently reversible by a later unrelated import. Auto-categorization at import time (CSV or sync) can now be undone per-batch, reverting just the categorization without discarding the rest of the import. The app also runs in Docker now (still on SQLite — see the [Docker](#docker) section below). See [PLAN.md](./PLAN.md) and [CHANGELOG.md](./CHANGELOG.md).

## Stack

- **Next.js 16** (App Router + Turbopack) · **React 19** · **TypeScript**
- **Tailwind v4** · **shadcn/ui** (base-nova style, Base UI primitives)
- **better-sqlite3** + **Drizzle ORM** — local SQLite file at `./data/money.db`
- **Recharts** for the dashboard trend chart (client-side only)
- **Vitest** for parser/categorization/sync/accounts unit tests · GitHub Actions runs two jobs on every PR: `test` (lint + test + build) and `docker` (compose build, boot the container, round-trip `db:export`/`db:import`)

No auth. No Plaid. No cloud — this runs on your machine (or your own Docker host), and your ledger never leaves it. The one outbound call the app makes is a read-only pull from SimpleFIN, and what comes back is written to the local SQLite file.

### Docker

An alternative to `pnpm dev`, still on SQLite — no Postgres yet (see `docs/plans/dockerize-postgres.md`). The ledger lives in a named Docker volume rather than `./data`, so it isn't directly visible on the host; use the scripts below rather than reaching into the volume.

```bash
sudo mkdir -p backups && sudo chmod 777 backups  # Linux only, and only before the FIRST run — see note below
pnpm db:seed-volume            # first run only, BEFORE `docker compose up` — copies ./data/money.db
                                # into the volume so the container doesn't start with an empty ledger
docker compose up -d           # http://127.0.0.1:3000 — bound to loopback only, this app has no auth
```

On real Linux hosts, Docker auto-creates a missing `./backups` bind-mount directory as root-owned, which the container's unprivileged user can't write to. The `mkdir`/`chmod` step must come **before** `db:seed-volume` — `db:seed-volume` starts a container of its own to verify the seed, which would otherwise race Docker into auto-creating `./backups` as root first. `chmod` rather than `chown`ing to a specific user: both the container (writing snapshots) and `pnpm db:export` running on the host (copying them out) need write access to the same directory, and they run as different users. Not needed on macOS Docker Desktop, whose bind-mount layer doesn't have this issue.

`.env.local` is optional: SimpleFIN sync degrades to a configuration banner without it, and CSV import works either way. `TZ` is required (`compose.yaml` sets `America/Los_Angeles`; the container refuses to boot without one, since the app derives the current budget month from local time).

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `pnpm db:export` | Snapshot the running container's ledger out to `./backups/` |
| `pnpm db:import <file>` | Stop the container, restore a snapshot file, restart |
| `pnpm db:seed-volume` | One-time host → volume copy (run before the first `docker compose up`) |
| `pnpm db:backfill-merchants` | Bring stored merchant names in line with the current normalizer, and rewrite the trained rules that key off them. Dry run by default; `--apply` snapshots first and writes in one transaction. Runs inside the container, so rebuild the image (`docker compose build && docker compose up -d`) after a normalizer change before running it |

Create an account (name, type, balance + date) from `/import` — checking, savings, credit card, or loan. A card or a loan asks for the **balance owed** as a positive number, and a card can carry a credit limit and a minimum payment; a loan carries neither, on purpose. Liabilities have no transaction feed, so the rest of this section is about the two accounts that do. Debt lives on [`/accounts`](#accounts-and-net-worth) instead.

### Automated sync (the normal path)

```bash
SIMPLEFIN_SETUP_TOKEN=<token from simplefin.org> pnpm simplefin:claim
```

That runs once. It exchanges the setup token for a long-lived access URL and writes `SIMPLEFIN_ACCESS_URL` to `.env.local` with owner-only permissions — the URL carries your credentials, so it is gitignored and only its host is ever displayed.

Then open `/sync`, pick which remote account each local account maps to, and hit **Sync now**. Posted transactions are written straight to the ledger — no preview step — behind a database snapshot taken first. The page also shows:

- **Balance check** — the bank's balance next to the one this ledger computes. A difference is only flagged as a real drift once the bank's own figure is dated after your newest ledger row; a same-day, older, or dateless bank figure shows as unconfirmed instead, alongside its as-of date, since it may just not have caught up yet. Available balance is listed separately; that gap is where pending card holds live.
- **Transfers needing review** — the rare same-day, same-amount transfer the matcher can't resolve by counting. Pick the two halves yourself.
- **Reversals needing review** — a charge and its own cancellation, both on *one* account: a reversed transfer, a disputed charge with its provisional credit, a returned payment. No matcher can see these (they all require a pair to span two accounts), so this queue finds them and hands every one to you. It never links anything by itself, on purpose — the same shape shows up as coincidence often enough that auto-linking would delete real spending with no error, and a plain merchandise refund fits it too and should stay visible. Where a date and amount has several candidates, both dropdowns start on "Choose…" so a wrong pairing is never one click away, and **Not a reversal** dismisses one for good without linking it first. A row that both queues can claim says so in each; **linking** it clears it from both, while marking it "not a reversal" only answers this reading — the cross-account one stays.
- **Linked pairs** — what got paired, automatically for transfers and by hand for reversals, each with a **Not a transfer** button (**Not a reversal**, when both halves are on the same account). Pairing hides both rows from every spending view, so this is the way back out when a same-day, same-amount coincidence gets linked by mistake. The correction sticks: an unrelated later import landing on the same date can't silently re-link the same two rows, though either one can still pair correctly with a genuinely different match afterward.
- **Undo this sync** — deletes the last batch's rows and the batch itself, no dev-server restart needed. Only offered while that sync is still the newest import of any kind — a CSV import landed afterward can end up relying on one of its rows, so undo refuses once that's happened rather than deleting silently. The pre-write snapshot stays as the escape hatch.
- **Undo just the auto-categorization** — when a sync batch had rows auto-categorized by a trained rule, this page links to `/import/success/[batchId]` to revert just those categorizations, leaving the transactions (and any you've since hand-recategorized) alone. Use this instead of the full sync undo when only a rule's category choice was wrong.

Changing a link later — pointing an account at a different feed, or unlinking it — updates the connection and nothing else; it never touches your transactions. Each imported row remembers which feed sent it, so sync still recognises it wherever it sits. Two things do get flagged at that moment, because neither can be repaired automatically: transactions from the feed you are claiming that are already filed under a **different** account (sync will refuse to import them again, which is the de-dup working, but they stay where they are, so this account's balance runs short by that amount until you move or re-enter them), and any older rows imported before the app recorded which feed they came from, which no id-based duplicate check can ever match.

A sync also moves the balance of any linked liability that has no transactions of its own — the mortgage, in practice — without importing a row for it. That gets reported in the sync result ("Balance updated: …") even on a sync that imported nothing, so an anchor never moves quietly behind an "Already up to date" message.

A linked **credit card** is different: sync stages its charges and payments like any other account, but only rows dated after the card's own starting point — a row on or before it is dropped without a warning, since that's routine rather than exceptional. Once a card is importing its own rows, its balance is no longer feed-maintained; it needs the same hand-reconcile discipline as any other card from that point on. If you'd hand-entered any of that card's charges or payments before ever linking it, sync refuses to stage its transactions at all and tells you to remove those entries first — the bank's own record of the same charge would otherwise arrive later as an unrelated-looking second row and count twice. A mortgage still never imports transactions, regardless of linking — see [CLAUDE.md](./CLAUDE.md) for why.

SimpleFIN caps history at 90 days — the feed says so itself when you ask for more. Sync halves that to 45 as its own conservative limit, so anything older has to come from a CSV import; that is a property of the feed, not a missing feature. The feed also returns only posted rows, and sync refuses to write a pending one if it ever appears: there is no way to update a row once it posts, so it would freeze a pre-authorisation amount and duplicate the real charge.

### CSV import (older history)

Upload a Star One CSV export at `/import`. The preview shows row counts, duplicates, pending rows, and errors; clicking **Confirm import** snapshots the DB, inserts the batch inside a transaction, and links transfer pairs across accounts. If the snapshot degrades to a plain file copy, the import still completes, but the success page shows a warning instead of silently trusting it as a working rollback point. This is the only way to load anything the feed no longer carries.

Each **asset** account on `/import` also has an inline "start [balance] on [date]" form — the way to fix a starting-balance anchor that was set wrong (or left at the created-with-$0 default), since a CSV import can only ever move the anchor forward, never correct a too-late one. The date is capped at today. That form takes a *signed* balance with no relabelling, so it is deliberately closed to cards and loans (server-side, not just hidden): a liability's anchor is only ever moved from `/accounts`, where the wording and the sign flip are handled for you. CSV import refuses a liability target for the same reason.

Rows a trained rule auto-categorizes on the way in (from either CSV import or sync) can be undone independently of the import itself: when the success page has revertible rows, an "Undo auto-categorization" button reverts just those categorizations — a transaction you've since hand-recategorized is left alone even if it landed back on the same category a rule chose.

Optional: `pnpm simplefin:sample` dumps a live account payload to `.context/simplefin-sample.json` when you want to inspect what the feed actually returns.

### Accounts and net worth

`/accounts` is where debt is managed. Assets and liabilities are two ruled lists, with a `Cash` subtotal, a `Debt` subtotal, and net worth on the bottom line. The page renders whether or not you have any debt yet.

A credit card carries a credit limit, a minimum payment, and a monthly paydown goal, all editable after the fact, and its row shows utilization against that limit, when the balance was last confirmed, and how much you paid it down this month. "Paid down" counts money that actually came from another account: a credit landing on the card and paired with a charge on the *same* card is a reversal, not a payment, and no longer inflates that figure. Set a paydown goal and the same line becomes "paid down $X of $Y planned this month" — a standing target compared against what you actually paid, independent of the minimum payment. Four things move a liability's balance. Reconcile and Refresh are mutually exclusive — a row offers exactly one of them, never both and never neither — while the other two are card-only and sit alongside whichever it got:

- **Reconcile** — type what the liability actually owes today, as a positive number, and (on a card) a "You owe" / "You're owed" toggle next to it says which direction — a card can genuinely be owed money after an overpayment. The normal way a card's balance moves, and the only way for any liability that isn't feed-linked — including a car loan. A loan or mortgage never offers the toggle at all; it can never legitimately carry a positive balance.
- **Refresh** — pull the balance straight from the bank feed. Offered only where it can work: a feed-linked account with no transactions of its own, which in practice means the mortgage. It refreshes balances only; it does not import transactions.
- **Add a charge** (or a refund) — a hand-entered, categorized transaction, so card spending counts toward its envelope in the month you spent it. A charge dated on or *before* your last reconcile is refused, because that reconcile already includes it and counting it again would be spending that never moved the balance; the refusal hands you to Reconcile instead. When that leaves no valid date at all — the balance you're working from is dated today — the button is hidden rather than offered and then refused. Anything you typed in by hand can be taken back out again from the transaction list's row menu ("Remove this charge", behind a confirmation, and only ever on rows you typed — a transaction from your bank can't be removed this way). Adding the first hand-entered charge to a card whose balance comes from the bank switches that card to Reconcile, which the dialog tells you before you save; removing it hands the balance back to the bank. Once a linked card starts importing its own transactions (below), this is refused outright instead: your bank is about to report the same charge, so a hand-typed one would double it.
- **Mark a transaction as a card payment** — from the row menu on `/transactions`, pairing the money leaving checking with the money landing on the card. Paired, it counts as neither spending nor income. Unmark it the same way ("Not a card payment," offered only on a pair this app actually created — not on an ordinary bank-to-bank transfer, which it could never undo). Refused once the card imports its own transactions — the bank's own payment row is coming, so a hand-made pairing would double it.
- **Link to a card charge** — the fix for the case above, as of v1.2.0. Once the card imports its own transactions, this item on the row menu lets you pick the real bank row a checking payment matches, even when the two post days apart. It removes both from spending, like any transfer, and warns rather than blocks when the checking side was already filed under a category — that category's spend total is about to change.

Reconcile and Refresh each record the balance they replaced, and the row offers to go back to it. That undo is itself undoable, so it's a toggle rather than a one-shot. Only one prior balance is kept, though — there's no history series behind it, which is also why saving a reconcile that changes nothing is treated as a no-op rather than spending that single slot on it. (A charge or a card payment moves the balance without touching the anchor, so there's nothing for those two to record.)

**A linked card can import its own transactions (v1.1.0).** Once a card is linked to your bank feed, sync stages its charges and payments the same way it does for checking or savings — categorized, attributed by merchant, counted toward your budget. The one boundary: only rows dated strictly *after* the card's own starting point come in, ever, so the history underneath a payment you already filed by hand never re-arrives and gets double-counted. That also means the card's balance is no longer bank-maintained — Refresh stops being offered the moment a card is linked, permanently, in favor of Reconcile — so a linked card needs the same occasional hand-reconcile any other card does. The automatic transfer matcher also stops treating a card row as a transfer candidate, so a card purchase can't accidentally auto-link against an unrelated same-day checking deposit.

A mortgage or a loan is deliberately quieter: no utilization bar, no hand-entered charges, no paid-down line until it has activity of its own. It sits under a **Long-term** heading in muted ink, counts toward net worth, and otherwise stays out of the way — it's a fact about your life, not a problem you're solving this month. It still gets a balance control, though: Refresh while it's feed-linked with no transactions, and Reconcile otherwise. A car loan you never linked would be uncorrectable without it.

## Core data rules

These are load-bearing — the whole app is built around them:

1. **All money is stored as signed integer `amount_cents`.** Never floats. Withdrawals negative, deposits positive. **A liability's balance is stored negative too** — owing $2,000 on a Visa is `-200000`. You always type the amount as a positive number; a direction (which account creation always assumes, and which Reconcile offers as a "You owe" / "You're owed" toggle on a card) says which way the sign goes, through the one function both paths share, so they can't disagree about it. The point of the negative convention is that nothing downstream needed a new branch: an overdrawn checking account already produced negative balances, so every balance sum, transfer-pair check, spending filter and accounting-parens formatter kept working as written. **"Spent" is that same signed sum** — a refund reduces the category's spend on every page — and what counts as spending is decided by the category's kind (income/expense/fund), never by which way the amount points. An amount-sign filter in this codebase is rarely only a sign filter; check what else it is quietly excluding before touching one.
2. **The CSV's signs are already correct.** `Amount Debit` is pre-negative, `Amount Credit` is positive. No `Math.abs`, no sign flips by description. (This is the bug Plaid users keep hitting.)
3. **Dedup is `(account_id, import_batch_id, import_row_hash)`**, never Star One's `Transaction Number` — they reuse `6098` for pending deposits across rows. `import_row_hash = sha1(date | amount_cents | raw_description | raw_memo | row_index)`. Feed rows have no row index, so they dedup on the bank's own `external_id` instead, enforced by a partial unique index on `(simplefin_source_account_id, external_id)` — scoped by the feed that issued the id, not by which local account happens to hold the row, because re-pointing an account's feed link moves the account but never the row's origin. The feed also re-sends days you already imported from CSV, so sync compares content signatures too — counted as a multiset, so two genuinely identical same-day coffees both survive. Memo whitespace is normalised on both sides of that comparison, because the CSV parser preserves Star One's padding byte-for-byte while the feed sends the same row trimmed.
4. **Transfer-pair detection is memo-independent.** Two rows pair iff `|txn_a - txn_b| == 1` AND same date AND `|amount_a| == |amount_b|` AND opposite signs AND different accounts. Star One labels the receiving-side memo correctly only ~20% of the time, so memo is confirmation-only. The feed carries no transaction number, so sync pairs by counting instead: bucket on `(date, |amount|)`, keep only opposite-signed rows in different accounts, and auto-link any bucket where the two sides balance, since every possible pairing excludes the same rows from spending. As of v1.1.0 a card row is never a candidate in this matcher at all — a card purchase and an unrelated same-day checking deposit would otherwise form a balanced bucket and auto-link away real spending. Unbalanced buckets are the ones `/sync` asks you about — as is any pair with a CSV leg carrying a transaction number the stronger ±1 matcher already judged and declined, whether the other leg is from the feed or CSV too. A still-pending CSV row can never become a pair member (the feed itself never carries pending rows at all), and a pair you've explicitly marked "Not a transfer" stays rejected — it won't get silently re-linked by a later import, though either row can still pair with a genuinely different match. **"Different accounts" holds for every automatic matcher, with one human-only exception:** a charge and its cancellation on the *same* account (a reversed transfer, a provisional credit) can be paired by hand from `/sync`'s reversal queue. Nothing pairs those automatically — the shape is coincidence often enough that an algorithm would delete real spending — and the same-account permission is an argument the queue's own action passes, never anything a form can set.
5. **Every batch import writes a DB snapshot first** to `data/money.db.pre-import-{timestamp}`, using `VACUUM INTO` so WAL-resident writes are included — a plain file copy could produce a snapshot that would not open. Last 10 are kept, pruned only once the write commits. Rollback = stop dev server, swap file. Sync snapshots too, and adds a logical undo that deletes just that batch without stopping the server. Both paths check whether the snapshot actually came back consistent; if it degraded to a plain copy, a warning is persisted on the batch and shown on its success page instead of assuming the rollback works.
6. **Money comes in as decimal strings from the feed** — parse with `parseAmountToCents` (string math), never `parseFloat(x) * 100`.
7. **The merchant name a row groups under is computed once, when the row is written** — never recomputed on read. That makes changing `src/lib/normalize.ts` a two-step job: the code alone leaves every stored name a generation behind, so trained rules keep matching names nothing writes anymore while new imports write names no rule matches. `pnpm db:backfill-merchants` is the second step. It recomputes each name from the original bank memo (not from the existing name), rewrites `category_rules` by following the rows rather than by renormalizing the rule's own value (it falls back to renormalizing the value itself only for a rule whose name no row carries any more), and carries dismissed subscriptions across too — they're stored by merchant name with no link back to the rows, so a rename would otherwise resurrect a dismissed subscription with no way to dismiss it again. A `contains` rule is the one thing no backfill can repair: its value is a substring, so if a normalizer change stops that word appearing in any name, the rule is dead for good. Links are the third consumer and the one nothing can rewrite: a `/transactions?merchant=` URL filters on the exact stored name, so a bookmark or a shared link built before a backfill points at a name that no longer exists. That one is handled at the far end instead — a merchant filter matching nothing names the key it was given and offers a substring search as the way back.
8. **A category's `kind` (income/expense/fund) can't change once it's been used** — any transaction or planned amount locks it in, with one narrow exception: an expense-labeled category that turns out to be income can be reclassified back, but only if every one of its transactions is positive. Archiving a category excludes it from every picker and stops its auto-categorization rules, but never deletes it — a month it already has a planned or spent amount in keeps showing it (so archiving never erases that month's numbers), and it disappears from a month's view only once there's nothing left there to reconcile.

## What's NOT in V1

Auth. Cloud sync of your data. Multi-currency. Bill pay. Investment tracking. Tax features. Split transactions. YNAB-style overspend-shuffle. Cloud/NAS hosting (Docker exists for local self-hosting only — see [docs/plans/dockerize-postgres.md](./docs/plans/dockerize-postgres.md), PR3).

Credit cards were on this list until v0.16.0 and came in. What deliberately stayed out of them, since it reads as an omission otherwise: no interest or APR modeling, no payoff projection, no statement periods or due dates, no minimum-payment *tracking* (the number is stored for reference and never compared against what you actually paid — the separate, optional paydown goal added in v1.5.0 is what gets compared), and no balance history — an account remembers exactly one prior balance, which is enough to undo a reconcile and not enough to draw a debt trend line. Automated import of card transactions came in with v1.1.0 for a *linked* card, from the day it was reconciled forward — an unlinked card, or one your bank doesn't expose over SimpleFIN at all, still takes charges by hand. A mortgage never imports transactions at all, regardless of linking: see [CLAUDE.md](./CLAUDE.md) for why.
